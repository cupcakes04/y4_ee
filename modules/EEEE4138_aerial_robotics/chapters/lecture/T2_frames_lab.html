Move φ, then θ, then ψ. Which rings move each time?
        
check
φ: no ring moves, only the drone. θ: only the red φ ring tilts. ψ: the orange and red rings both swing round.
        An angle never moves its own ring (spinning a ring about its own axis leaves it looking the same), and it carries every ring that comes after it in the chain ψ → θ → φ.
        So ψ's axis k_v is fixed to the world, θ's axis j_v1 rides on the yaw, and φ's axis i_b rides on everything (it's the nose).
        It's a physical gimbal: outer ring = yaw, middle = pitch, inner = roll (the slide's gimbal-lock video). Gimbal lock is the middle ring turning the inner ring flat onto the outer one.

  1. Where: for ZYX (ψ-θ-φ), at pitch θ = ±90°.
    2. 
          
  2. Picture: the roll axis i_b swings onto the yaw axis k_v, so the φ ring and the ψ ring lie in the same plane. Two angles do one job, and one direction of rotation is lost.
    3. 
          
  3. Equation: [p q r]ᵀ = M[φ̇ θ̇ ψ̇]ᵀ with det M = cos θ. At cos θ = 0, columns 1 and 3 are parallel, so M can't be inverted. The Euler-rate map has tan θ and 1/cos θ, which go to ∞.
    4. 
          
  4. Effect on R: at θ = 90°, R depends only on φ − ψ (θ = −90°: on φ + ψ), so φ and ψ can't be recovered separately (Tutorial 1 Q5–Q6).
