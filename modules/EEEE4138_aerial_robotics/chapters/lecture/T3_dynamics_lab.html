Yaw rate r spins the drone about z. It doesn't make w (motion along z) change. The cross product decides which component changes: r × (motion along x) → a change along y (panel 2).

1 · The four blocks (Tutorial 2 Q4 = your StateDerivative)

Kinematics describes motion: pure geometry, turning one rate into another, with no m, J, F or M (sl.3). Dynamics explains why motion changes: forces and moments, divided by m or J. Click a block.

Answer shape: "draw the block diagram / list the states" (Tutorial 2 Q4) added (layout); content from T2 Q4 solution
  1. 12 states in 4 vectors: position [pN pE pD], body velocity [u v w], Euler angles [φ θ ψ], body rates [p q r].
  2. Inputs: total body force F = [Fx Fy Fz] and moment M = [Mx My Mz]. Parameters: m, J.
  3. Four blocks, each feeding an integrator 1/s, with the "needs" signals fed back from the states (state feedback inside the model, not a controller).
  4. Every integrator needs an initial condition X(0) = X₀ (e.g. hover: position, zero velocity, level, zero rates).

2 · ω × v: why u and v change when the axes turn

Top-down view, N up and E right. The drone yaws at rate r. Newton works in NED: no force ⇒ the NED velocity doesn't change (straight path), even though the nose swings round. Body axes rotate under that fixed velocity, so u and v change. That bookkeeping is the −ω × v term (sl.8, 11–13).

3 · ω × (Jω): panel 2 again, but for spinning

      
Panel 2's rule was: with no force, the velocity stays fixed in NED, and the body-axis numbers u, v change only because the axes turn. Rotation has exactly the same rule with one swap: with no moment, the angular momentum h = Jω (the "amount of spin", like m·v is the amount of motion) stays fixed in NED, and the body-axis numbers p, q, r change because the axes turn.

"equal J", then play. Do p, q, r change? Does the purple spin axis move?
        
check
Nothing changes. With equal J, h = Jω points straight along ω, so ω × h = 0: there's no bookkeeping term, and the spin just carries on. "Spin about z only" is the same for unequal J, since h is along ω there too.

      
"sl.24 rates, no moment" (q = 1, r = 2, M = 0), then play. Watch the orange arrow and the "h in NED" line, then p, q, r.
        
check
h stays fixed in NED (the same three numbers the whole time), exactly like panel 2's NED velocity. But p, q, r keep changing: p starts at ṗ = (Jy − Jz)qr/Jx = (−0.010)(1)(2)/0.020 = −1 rad/s² with no moment at all. That's the coupling term: the body turns underneath a fixed h, so its body components move around.

      
"phone flip": a spin mostly about y (the middle inertia) with a tiny bit of p. What happens? added
        
check
The tiny p grows and the drone flips over again and again, with q reversing sign. That's the coupling term amplifying a small error (the "tennis racket" effect). Try it by tossing your phone spinning about its middle axis. Spin about x or z (the smallest or largest J) is steady instead. Not in the slides, but it's the same equation.
