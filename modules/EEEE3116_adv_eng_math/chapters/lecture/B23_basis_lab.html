B2–B3 basis vectors and Gram–Schmidt lab

One idea runs through every panel: a coefficient is a shadow. Dotting x with a unit axis tells you how much of x lies along that axis. That only adds back up to x when the axes are perpendicular. Gram–Schmidt is how you make them perpendicular.

Source: EEEE3116 B2 “Data, vectors and transformations” sl.4–24, B3 “Gram–Schmidt” sl.6–21, and the Section B practical Q1. Anything not from the slides is tagged added.

1 · A coefficient is a shadow

Rotate the two axes. The dashed drops land on each axis: that's X₁ and X₂. The dotted path adds X₁B₁ + X₂B₂ back up.

check

0. (4,4) points at 45° and B₂ points at 135°: 90° apart, so no shadow. The point has none of axis 2 in it. That's what “correlation = 0” means on sl.21.

Set the gap to 60° (the axes are no longer perpendicular). Does the dotted path still land on x?
check

No. Each shadow now also contains part of the other axis, so adding them double-counts. That's why B must be orthogonal for x = BᵀX, and it's exactly the CDMA leak in panel 5. Gram–Schmidt (panel 3) is the fix.

3 · Gram–Schmidt: subtract the shadow

Step through it. Step 2 is panel 1's shadow again, this time of v₂ on u₁. Default is B3 sl.10: v₁ = (5, 2), v₂ = (1, 7).

u₁ = v₁v₂shadow αu₁u₂ = v₂ − αu₁
step:
Turn v₂ until it lies on v₁'s line. What happens to u₂?
check

u₂ shrinks to 0: v₂ is all shadow, nothing sticks out. The two vectors were never independent, so there's no second direction to find. In QR this shows up as a 0 on R's diagonal, the same “rank” failure as A2.

Make v₂ exactly perpendicular to v₁. What is α?
check

0. There's no shadow, so nothing to subtract, and u₂ = v₂.

Two, one per earlier axis. Removing only the u₁ shadow would leave part of v₃ along u₂, so the result would not be ⟂ u₂. In general vⱼ loses j − 1 shadows (B3 sl.13).

On the sl.14 example, why do the u's come out as plain x, z, y axes?
check

The slide picked easy numbers. v₁ is already the x axis, and v₂ = (2, 0, 3) minus its x part leaves (0, 0, 3), the z axis. Switch to tilted to see the general case, where none of the u's are axis-aligned.

5 · CDMA: why two users leak into each other (practical Q1)

Both nodes transmit at once: r = s₁c₁ + s₂c₂, where s = amplitude × bit. The receiver reads A's bit with a shadow, z₁ = (r·c₁)/|c₁|².

Practical codes, bit B = −1. Slide B's amplitude up from 0. At what amplitude does A's plain reading flip to the wrong sign?
check

Above 2. The leak is s₂ (c₁·c₂)/|c₁|² = −amp × 2/4, which beats A's +1 once amp > 2. The practical uses 3 (a near, strong node), so A is misread. Switch to the perpendicular codes and the leak is 0 at any amplitude.

The fix (Q1.3–4): Gram–Schmidt the codes into perpendicular q₁, q₂, so that C = QR. Then Qᵀr = Rs is a triangular system, and back-substitution gives s exactly. See md Part 6 for the numbers.
