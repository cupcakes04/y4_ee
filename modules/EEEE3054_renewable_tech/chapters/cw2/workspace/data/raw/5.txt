point 1: 
Solar photovoltaic systems generally incur relatively low operating and maintenance (O&M) costs compared with their upfront capital investment. Industry and project-finance references commonly estimate annual O&M expenditure at approximately 1%-3% of initial capital cost, depending on system size, site conditions, and maintenance requirements

source: https://pv-maps.com/en/blog/solar-om-costs-per-mw
The OPEX (Operating Expenditures) of a photovoltaic plant typically represents between 1.5% and 3% of the initial CAPEX per year. For a €10M plant, this means €150,000-300,000 annually over 25-30 years. If you don’t optimize these costs, you could be leaving €1-2 million in net present value on the table.
Assuming annual O&M costs equal 1.5%-2.0% of CAPEX, an $8,500 solar PV system would require approximately $128-$170 per year for maintenance (author calculation based on industry O&M benchmarks).

point 2:
PV systems operating in arid and desert environments experience elevated dust accumulation, making periodic cleaning an important maintenance activity. Studies from desert climates report significant energy losses when modules are not cleaned regularly

source: extension://bfdogplmndidlpjfhoijckpakkdjkkil/pdf/viewer.html?file=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.pv-tech.org%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2Flegacy-publication-pdfs%2F7214fc38b9-pv-soiling-in-dry-climates-causes-impacts-and-solutions.pdf
A desert-climate PV study from Qatar found:

"In dry climates soiling rates are typically in the order of 0.1-1% per day."

The same study states:

"At the OTF, soiling causes the power of PV modules ... to decrease by 10-20% per month."

source: https://www.nlr.gov/news/detail/features/2021/scientists-studying-solar-try-solving-a-dusty-problem
The energy lost annually from soiling amounts to as much as 7% in parts of the United States to as high as 50% in the Middle East.
