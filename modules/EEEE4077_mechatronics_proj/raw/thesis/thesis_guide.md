# MEng FYP Thesis

> Note: I personally do not care of my marks, just fyi i guess, so no need bias my work for this.

This assessment aims to demonstrate your achievements in your project in a professional document, describing how you delivered on your target specification and the engineering choices you have made in doing so. The project is an enormous undertaking, and you will have invested significant time. To be successful, this effort needs to be well structured, with explicit reference to the marking rubric. A good thesis will allow the reader to accurately judge the progress and decisions made during the course of your project.

You will present a clear specification for what needs to be achieved in your project and underpin your chosen methods to meet the specification successfully, how you validated this success, and your reflections on project management and your learning journey as a professional engineer.

This is your final written report that shows:

- What you achieved in your project.
- How you met your specification.
- The engineering decisions you made along the way.

Your Thesis should be professional and well-structured, allowing the reader to clearly understand your progress and choices.

Use the assessment rubric to guide your writing and content.

> You must hit “Submit” on your final submission for your work to be accepted!

## Introduction 0%

Ungraded, but required for completeness of the document (see rubric “Quality of Communication and Completeness”)
Should contain a brief background to the project, the aim, specification points, and methodology.
These can be transferred from your planning report.

## Design and Implementation 40%

This is the main body of the document. Here, you are expected to explain what you have designed and developed.

A tutorial of steps undertaken in the project is not needed. It would be best to document your engineering decisions and their justifications. (It may help to imagine your reader as someone who wants to reproduce your project but using different tools – e.g. a different programming language – your exact code doesn’t help them much, but the reasoning and explanation of how/why it works does).

> Note: justification means using literature to support your working.

- `System modelling`
    - Modelling should be part of your project; for some projects, this is immediately evident (e.g. PLECS simulation of a power converter). For others, this may feel like a challenge. Using a purely software project as an example, you may model how users would interact with the system and then use this model later to support the testing of your system.
    - Modelling includes basic analytical calculations.
- `System development`
    - This part of the rubric evaluates the development work you’ve done to make your project a reality. This should follow your planned methodology, with an explanation/justification of where you have deviated.
    - For most projects, the content of this section should be apparent, but in some cases, there will be a substantial overlap with the “System modelling” section. This is fine, but you should discuss the structure of this section with your supervisor to ensure it works for your project. (For example, in a purely simulation-based project, your development may involve further refining the model to reflect real-world operation).
- `Use of literature to support development`
    - References should be used throughout the section to support your work; you should clarify how the referenced material has been used.
    - Literature includes company white papers, tutorials, software libraries, etc. (not just academic research papers and textbooks).

## Final System Testing and Validation 30%

This section demonstrates that the specifications you set out for the project have been met. You must display results in an intuitive, understandable, and engaging way.

- `Specification Validation`
    - The sections within the rubric are quite self-explanatory for this one. The specific analysis you apply will depend on your project and should be discussed with your supervisor (you should also have some ideas from your planning report).
- `Presentation of testing setup and results`
    - As part of your testing, you should explain the testing setup; for example, this could be a circuit on test with the equipment you are using for measurement or a block diagram of the process you will follow to test your software.
    - The remaining criteria are concerned with the quality of your presentation of results and discussion.

## Conclusion 25%

- `Consideration of the system within the wider context`
    - You should start this section by summarising the current state of your project (i.e. what specification points have been completed).
    - In this part, you must assess and align your project with the broader literature. For example, it might make sense to compare similar products to show the benefits of your work.
    - Note: You are primarily looking at the current state of your project and should start by summarising the completion of the planned specification points (perhaps in a table).
    - You should aim to take a comprehensive look and answer the question, “What is the impact of your work?”
    - You should also discuss potential future work and the impact this could have.
- `Reflection on Management`
    - In this section, you must reflect on how the project went (from a management perspective). Use your risks and mitigations from the planning report as a starting point – were these suitable? Did anything unplanned happen?
    - What learnings will you take away from future projects? (this might be things that went well or mitigations for things that went wrong).

## Communication Quality and Overall Completeness of Document 5%

This is self-explanatory. Make it look like a professional document.

# Moderator Defence

Following submitting your FYP Thesis, you will meet with your moderator for your Defence interview. **You must arrange this** to convene at a mutually convenient time. Please refer to the FYP Important Deadlines document posted on this Moodle page.

The accompanying presentation you prepare should contain as many slides as necessary to communicate your project in 20 minutes. The remainder of your Defence interview will comprise structured questions from your Moderator regarding your project's technical, theoretical, and management aspects.

## [Moderator Only] Dissertation/Thesis Defence 10%

This is a combination of demonstration of your project to your moderator as well as being questioned by them with respect to the points in the rubric.

Think about (and discuss with your supervisor) what the best method of demonstrating your project is - you may invite your moderator to conduct this in the student labs (please make this clear when booking your Defence interview).
