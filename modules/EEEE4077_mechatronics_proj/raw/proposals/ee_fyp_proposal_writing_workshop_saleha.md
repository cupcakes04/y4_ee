## Slide 2: Workshop Aims
By the end of the session you should be able to:
- Identify the key components of an engineering project proposal.
- Structure a clear and logical proposal outline that organises ideas coherently and reflects disciplinary expectations.

---

## Slide 3: Project Overview
Key components required in the project overview section:

1. **Project purpose and context:**
   - Clearly describe the project’s purpose / problem statement and its relevance in academic or industrial settings.
2. **Key elements and interrelations:**
   - Introduce key project elements and explain how they interact within the system or workflow.
3. **Visual aid for understanding:**
   - Use block diagrams to visually represent the system architecture and enhance comprehension of relationships.
4. **Supporting literature:**
   - Reference relevant literature to underpin the project’s background and rationale effectively.

---

## Slide 4: Project Specification
Key principles when formulating the project specification:

1. **SMART objectives:**
   - Setting Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound objectives ensures project clarity and feasibility.
2. **Stretch goals:**
   - Stretch goals provide flexibility and innovation opportunities when the project progresses ahead of schedule.
3. **Literature support:**
   - Supporting objectives with literature validates choices and aligns the project with current trends and needs.

---

## Slide 5: Assessment & Deliverables
Framework for aligning objectives to concrete deliverables and grading:

1. **Mapping objectives to deliverables:**
   - Each project objective is clearly linked to specific deliverables to ensure alignment with goals.
2. **Assessment methods:**
   - Outline assessment methods used to evaluate each deliverable, ensuring transparency and accountability.
3. **Tracking progress and performance:**
   - A clear framework is provided to track progress and measure project performance against expected outcomes.

---

## Slide 6: Methodology & Tools
Approach to detailing technical execution:

1. **Research methodologies:**
   - The project uses qualitative, quantitative, and experimental research approaches based on project needs.
2. **Development processes:**
   - Project execution is guided by structured processes.
3. **Essential tools & software:**
   - Key software tools like MATLAB, Python, and CAD are used to support project development and analysis.
4. **Literature support:**
   - Chosen methods and tools are validated and supported by relevant academic and industry literature.

---

## Slide 7: Originality
Demonstrating intellectual novelty and positioning:

1. **Unique project features:**
   - The project stands out by introducing unique elements that differentiate it from prior work.
2. **Building on existing work:**
   - It contributes new insights by either enhancing or diverging from existing research or solutions.
3. **Context and impact:**
   - The project is positioned within current industry or research, highlighting its relevance and potential impact.
4. **Visual comparison:**
   - A comparison chart contrasts existing work with the project, illustrating the innovation effectively.

## Slide 8: Risk Management (Example)
A risk register table format assessing likelihood, impact, and mitigation:

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Equipment failure** | Medium | High | Have backup equipment or service contracts |
| **Ordering delays** | High | Medium | Order early and track delivery timelines |
| **Software bugs** | Medium | High | Implement version control and regular testing |
| **Team availability** | Low | Medium | Schedule buffer time and cross-train team members |

*(Note: Risks must focus on technical and operational project delivery risks rather than generic workplace health & safety).*

## Slide 10: Other Considerations
Key report parameters:
- **Length:** 7–8 pages (excluding Cover page, Table of Contents, References, and Appendices).
- **Label tables and figures:** Ensure formal numbering, captions, and in-text cross-referencing.
- **Academic tone:** Maintain formal, precise technical and academic writing throughout.
