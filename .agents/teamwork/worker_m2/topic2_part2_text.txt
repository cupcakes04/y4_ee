Why quaternions?
At pitch θ = ±90°, roll and yaw are coupled.
cos θ = 0 when θ = 90 deg.
This can occur during extreme manoeuvres.
Unit quaternions avoid this Euler-angle singularity.
Quaternions can simplify attitude calculations.
They support algebraic rotation calculations. Computational cost 
depends on the operation and implementation.

Quaternion meaning: axis and angle
A rotation has an axis and an angle θ about that axis.
A unit quaternion stores this using four numbers, scalar first.
q = [cos(θ/2), a sin(θ/2)]
a is a unit vector along the rotation axis.
The four components have squared sum 1.
q and −q describe the same orientation.

Example: normalisation and inverse
Given q = [2, 0, 0, 2], calculate its norm.
‖q‖ = √(2² + 0² + 0² + 2²) = √8
Divide every component by √8:
q_unit = [1/√2, 0, 0, 1/√2]
For this unit quaternion, the inverse is its conjugate:
q_unit⁻¹ = q_unit* = [1/√2, 0, 0, −1/√2]

Angular rates
The components 𝑝, q, and 𝑟 correspond to the angular rates defined in the body 
frame ℱ𝑏.
The angular positions (Euler angles) 𝝓, 𝜃, and Ψ are defined in three 
different frames:
• Roll angle 𝝓 is a rotation from ℱ𝑣2 to ℱ𝑏 about the iv2=ib axis;
• Pitch angle 𝜃 is a rotation from ℱ𝑣1 to ℱ𝑣2 about the jv1=jv2 axis; and
• Yaw angle Ψ is a rotation from ℱ𝑣 to ℱ𝑣1 about the kv=kv1 axis.
The body-frame angular rates can be expressed in terms of the derivatives of the 
Euler angles, provided that the proper rotational transformations are carried out.
